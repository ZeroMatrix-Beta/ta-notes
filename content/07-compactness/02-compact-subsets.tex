\subsection{Compact subsets}

A subset $K$ of a metric space $(X,d)$ is compact if the metric space $(K, d|_{K \times K})$ is
compact (\cref{def:compact_metric_space}). This is equivalent to the definition in the script:

\begin{definition}[Definition 9.62, quoted from the official lecture notes]
\label{def:sequential_topological_compactness}
Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space. A subset $K \subset X$ is called \textbf{compact} if one of the
following equivalent conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\alph*)}]
  \item \label{item:seq_compact_def963}
    $K$ is \textbf{sequentially compact}: every sequence $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ in $K$ has
    a subsequence that is convergent (\cref{def:convergent_sequence}) in $K$.
  \item \label{item:top_compact_def963}
    $K$ is \textbf{topologically compact}: every family of open sets
    (\cref{def:open_closed}) $\{U_i\}_{i \in I}$ that cover $K$, has a finite subcover.
  \item \label{item:complete_totally_bounded_def963}
    $K$ is \textbf{complete} (every Cauchy sequence contained in $K$ has a limit in $K$) and
    \textbf{totally bounded} (\cref{def:totally_bounded}): for every $r>0$ there exist finitely
    many $x_1,\dots,x_n \in K$ such that the balls $B_r(x_1),\dots,B_r(x_n)$ cover $K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{ainote}
Corsin's own citation of this definition (matching the numbering in an earlier printing of the
script) reads \qt{9.63} and quotes only the first two of the three equivalent conditions above;
in the current script (v1, 10 July 2024) this is \textbf{Definition 9.62}, and \emph{9.63} is
instead the unnumbered remark explaining why condition \textbf{(b)} is called \qt{topological}.
Corrected above, with the third condition restored. Nothing downstream was ever missing
mathematically, since this document already proves the three-way equivalence at
\cref{thm:sequential_topological_compactness_equivalence} and has total boundedness at
\cref{def:totally_bounded}. The citation was simply under-quoting its own source.
\end{ainote}
