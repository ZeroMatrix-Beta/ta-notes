\chapter{The Chain Rule and Directional Derivatives}
\label{ch:chain_rule}

% Transition: Claude Opus 5 (Medium)
Having encoded the differential as a matrix, we now ask how it interacts with composition of
functions --- the single most useful computational tool in this part. The same machinery gives
directional derivatives, and with them the standard trap of several-variable calculus: a function
can have every directional derivative at a point and still fail to be differentiable, or even
continuous, there. \Cref{sec:sufficient_condition_differentiability} states what does suffice.
