\chapter{Submanifolds, Tangent and Normal Spaces}
\label{ch:submanifolds}

% Transition: Claude Opus 5 (Medium)
The implicit function theorem said that a level set is locally a graph. This chapter takes that
as a definition. A submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a set that looks, near each of its points,
like a piece of $\mathbb{R}^m$. Three descriptions of such a set turn out to be equivalent (a
level set of a submersion, a local graph, a local parametrization), and picking the convenient
one is most of the work in practice. Each point then carries its own linear algebra: the tangent
space of directions along the submanifold, and the normal space orthogonal to it.
